\begin{abstract}
This letter investigates a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted
multiple-input single-output (MISO) integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)
system, where a base station (BS) simultaneously serves a near and a far user
via non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) while illuminating a sensing target.
We aim to maximize a weighted communication-and-sensing throughput by jointly
optimizing the NOMA power allocation and the RIS phase shifts, subject to
per-link communication and sensing quality-of-service (QoS) constraints. To
tackle the formulated non-convex joint optimization problem, a meta-learning
(MAML)-based deep-learning (DL) algorithm is proposed, in which a lightweight
network predicts the power allocation from channel features while the RIS phase
shifts are refined per channel realization by an unrolled inner optimizer, and
second-order meta-gradients couple the two stages to improve generalization.
Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed algorithm drives the joint
QoS-violation rate from $29.4\%$ to $0.1\%$, scales the weighted sum-rate by
about $65\%$ as the RIS grows from $8$ to $64$ elements, and consistently
outperforms the orthogonal multiple access (OMA), no-RIS, and
partially-optimized baselines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{ntegrated} sensing and communication (ISAC) has emerged as a
key enabler for next-generation wireless networks, jointly designing the
sensing and communication functions on a shared spectral and spatial resource
to improve spectrum, energy, and hardware efficiency~\cite{liu2022isac,
deng2025mmwave}. When combined with non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA),
which superposes users in the power domain and separates them by successive
interference cancellation (SIC), ISAC can further raise the throughput of dense
Internet-of-Things deployments. However, severe blockage and path loss often
degrade both functions, motivating the use of a reconfigurable intelligent
surface (RIS) to reshape the propagation environment through low-cost passive
elements~\cite{wu2020ris}.

RIS-assisted ISAC resource management has attracted growing interest. In
\cite{wang2025hybrid}, a manifold-based scheme jointly optimized hybrid
beamforming and RIS phase shifts to shape the ISAC beampattern. In
\cite{lyu2024crb}, a penalty-based block-coordinate-descent method minimized the
Cram\'er--Rao bound for RIS-aided mmWave ISAC. A rate-splitting RIS-ISAC system
was studied in \cite{ke2025rsma}, where beamforming, rate-splitting, and RIS
phases were jointly designed to maximize throughput. For RIS-NOMA networks,
\cite{zou2023ml} introduced a deep-learning (DL) framework with model-agnostic
meta-learning (MAML) to jointly optimize the BS power allocation and RIS phase
shifts. Most of these designs, however, rely on per-instance alternating
optimization (AO), which incurs high computational complexity and is
ill-suited to the short channel-coherence times of practical links; learning
the high-dimensional RIS phase \emph{directly} with a network, on the other
hand, tends to overfit and to generalize poorly across channel
realizations~\cite{zou2023ml}.

To overcome these challenges, we propose a MAML-based DL framework for a MISO
RIS-NOMA-ISAC downlink that \emph{factorizes} the joint design according to its
dimensionality. The main contributions are summarized as follows. (i) We
formulate a weighted communication-plus-sensing throughput maximization problem
with explicit per-link QoS constraints, and contrast it with an OMA baseline.
(ii) We propose a bilevel solver in which a small network predicts the
low-dimensional NOMA power split—the quantity that benefits from learned
priors—while the high-dimensional RIS phase is refined per channel realization
by an unrolled inner loop, with second-order meta-gradients linking the two;
this factorization is the key to stable training and generalization. (iii)
Simulation results quantify the resulting NOMA, RIS, and learning gains and
confirm the ordering NOMA-RIS $>$ NOMA $>$ OMA-RIS $>$ OMA.

\subsection{System Model}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system}, we consider a downlink MISO RIS-ISAC
system in which a BS equipped with $M$
antennas, aided by an RIS of $N$ elements, simultaneously serves a near user
(n) and a far user (f), each with a single antenna, and illuminates a point
sensing target (T). The RIS applies a diagonal phase-shift matrix
$\bPhi=\mathrm{diag}(e^{j\phi_1},\dots,e^{j\phi_N})$ with unit-modulus
coefficients $\phi_i\in[0,2\pi)$.

\subsubsection{Channel Model}
With $\bG\in\Cset^{N\times M}$ the BS--RIS channel and $\bm{r}_k\in\Cset^{N}$
the RIS--user channel, the effective downlink channel of user
$k\in\{n,f\}$ is the cascade
\begin{equation}
\bh_k \;=\; \bG\herm \bPhi\, \bm{r}_k \;\in\;\Cset^{M}. \label{eq:cascade}
\end{equation}
Writing the cascade elementwise, the $m$-th antenna sees
$[\bh_k]_m=\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\bG]_{im}^{*}\,e^{j\phi_i}\,[\bm{r}_k]_i$, so each RIS
element contributes a phase-steered copy of the BS--RIS--user path and the
diagonal $\bPhi$ coherently combines the $N$ reflected paths at the user; this
is the standard cascaded (dyadic) RIS channel model of~\cite{wu2020ris,
zou2023ml}. All channels follow a Rician/Nakagami small-scale model with
distance-dependent large-scale path loss, consistent
with~\cite{sun2024noma}. In the RIS-aided case the direct BS--user path is
obstructed and the cascade~\eqref{eq:cascade} dominates; the \emph{no-RIS}
baseline retains only the (weak) direct link
$\bh_k^{\mathrm{dir}}$~\cite{zou2023ml}. The round-trip sensing response toward
the target is captured by the effective matrix $\bG_T\in\Cset^{M\times M}$,
which for a point target is the RIS-cascaded outer product
$\bG_T=\sqrt{\beta_T}\,(\bG\herm\bPhi\,\bm{h}_{TR})(\bm{h}_{RT}\herm\bPhi\bG)$
with round-trip gain $\beta_T$ and BS$\to$RIS$\to$target ($\bm{h}_{RT}$),
target$\to$RIS$\to$BS ($\bm{h}_{TR}$) links~\cite{lyu2024crb,wang2025hybrid};
since $\bG_T$ is rank-one, the optimal sensing receive combiner
$\bm{u}_T=\bG_T\bw_T/\|\bG_T\bw_T\|$ is obtained in closed form.

\subsubsection{NOMA Communication and Sensing Model}
The BS transmits the superposed signal
\begin{equation}
\bm{x}=\big(\sqrt{a_n P}\,s_n+\sqrt{a_f P}\,s_f\big)\bw_c
        +\sqrt{a_T P}\,\bw_T\, s_T ,
\end{equation}
where $P$ is the transmit power and $a_n+a_f+a_T=1$ is the power split over the
near/far/sensing streams. Here $\bw_c\in\Cset^{M}$ is the \emph{cluster}
(common) beamformer shared by the two superposed NOMA streams, and
$\bw_T\in\Cset^{M}$ is the dedicated sensing beamformer.

\emph{Cluster beamformer $\bw_c$.} In power-domain NOMA the near and far users
are served on a single spatial beam and separated by SIC, so one common
beamformer carries both data streams~\cite{zou2023ml,sun2024noma}. We steer it
toward the weaker (far) user, $\bw_c=\bh_f/\|\bh_f\|$, i.e.\ maximum-ratio
transmission (MRT) matched to $\bh_f$~\cite{deng2025mmwave}; this protects the
SIC-decoded far user, while the near user, enjoying a stronger channel, still
decodes reliably after cancelling the far stream. More generally $\bw_c$ can be
a weighted combination
$\bw_c\propto\beta\,\bh_f/\|\bh_f\|+(1-\beta)\,\bh_n/\|\bh_n\|$ that trades near-
against far-user gain; $\beta{=}1$ recovers the far-matched design used here.

\emph{Sensing beamformer $\bw_T$.} To keep the sensing waveform from leaking
onto the communication users, $\bw_T$ is placed in (or steered toward) the null
space of $[\bh_n,\bh_f]$ while maximizing illumination of the target, i.e.\ it
is the dominant eigenvector of
$\bG_T\herm\bG_T-\lambda\,[\bh_n,\bh_f][\bh_n,\bh_f]\herm$, a leakage-penalized
sensing design in the spirit of~\cite{wang2025hybrid,lyu2024crb}. With
$g_{kj}=|\bh_k\herm\bw_j|^2$ denoting the effective power gain from beamformer
$\bw_j$ onto link $k$, and assuming perfect SIC at the near user, the SINRs of
the far user, near user, and the sensing link are
\begin{align}
\gamma_f &= \frac{a_f g_{ff}P}{a_n g_{ff}P+a_T g_{fT}P+\sigma^2}, \label{eq:gf}\\
\gamma_n &= \frac{a_n g_{nn}P}{a_T g_{nT}P+\sigma^2}, \label{eq:gn}\\
\gamma_s &= \frac{a_T\,|\bm{u}_T\herm \bG_T \bw_T|^2\,P}{\sigma^2}, \label{eq:gs}
\end{align}
where $\sigma^2$ is the noise power. The scalar channel powers appearing
in~\eqref{eq:gf}--\eqref{eq:gs} are the effective (post-beamforming) gains
\begin{align}
g_{ff}&=|\bh_f\herm\bw_c|^2, & g_{nn}&=|\bh_n\herm\bw_c|^2, \notag\\
g_{fT}&=|\bh_f\herm\bw_T|^2, & g_{nT}&=|\bh_n\herm\bw_T|^2, \label{eq:gains}
\end{align}
in which $g_{ff}$ ($g_{nn}$) is the desired signal power delivered by the
cluster beam $\bw_c$ to the far (near) user, and $g_{fT}$ ($g_{nT}$) is the
cross-power that the sensing beam $\bw_T$ leaks onto the far (near) user;
$|\bm{u}_T\herm\bG_T\bw_T|^2$ is the round-trip sensing power after receive
combining by $\bm{u}_T$. Because $\bh_n,\bh_f$ and $\bG_T$ all depend on the RIS
phases through the cascade~\eqref{eq:cascade}, every gain
in~\eqref{eq:gains}—and hence every SINR—is a function of $\bm{\phi}$, which
couples the phase and power designs. The achievable rates are
$R_k=\log_2(1+\gamma_k)$, $k\in\{n,f,s\}$, and the weighted
communication-and-sensing throughput is
\begin{equation}
\Rdl=\omega_c\,(R_n+R_f)+\omega_s\,R_s, \label{eq:rdl}
\end{equation}
with weights $\omega_c=0.7$ and $\omega_s=0.3$. As a benchmark, the \emph{OMA}
scheme serves the near user, far user, and target in three orthogonal time
fractions $t_n+t_f+t_T=1$. Because the slots are orthogonal there is no
inter-stream interference, so each slot uses per-user matched beamforming
(MRT): $\bw_n=\bh_n/\|\bh_n\|$ in the near slot, $\bw_f=\bh_f/\|\bh_f\|$ in the
far slot, and the dominant eigenvector of $\bG_T\herm\bG_T$ (no leakage penalty
is needed since users are silent) in the sensing slot, each transmitted at the
full power $P$. This yields
$R_k^{\mathrm{OMA}}=t_k\log_2(1+\|\bh_k\|^2P/\sigma^2)$ for the users; OMA thus
trades the inter-user interference and SIC ceiling of NOMA for a pre-log time
penalty. Here the ``power allocation'' variable is the \emph{time-share}
$\bm{t}=(t_n,t_f,t_T)$ on the simplex, which plays the role of $\bm{a}$ in the
NOMA design and is predicted by the same learned map.

\emph{Access-scheme / RIS-configuration policies.} For a like-for-like
comparison, all four schemes (NOMA-RIS, NOMA no-RIS, OMA-RIS, OMA no-RIS) share
the resource-allocation and RIS policy of Table~\ref{tab:policy}: the
proposed learning solver optimizes the split ($\bm{a}$ or $\bm{t}$) and, when an
RIS is present, the phases $\bm{\phi}$; in the \emph{no-RIS} variants the
cascade is removed and only the residual direct link is used, so no phase design
is performed (equivalently $\bPhi{=}\bm{I}$)~\cite{zou2023ml}.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:prob}
We jointly optimize the power split $\bm{a}=(a_n,a_f,a_T)$ and the RIS phases
$\bm{\phi}=(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_N)$ to maximize the weighted throughput subject to
per-link QoS, yielding problem~(P1):
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:prob}
\begin{align}
\text{(P1)}\quad \max_{\bm{a},\,\bm{\phi}} \quad & \Rdl \label{eq:obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& R_n+R_f \;\ge\; R_{\mathrm{th},c}, \label{eq:qosc}\\
& R_s \;\ge\; R_{\mathrm{th},s}, \label{eq:qoss}\\
& a_n+a_f+a_T=1,\quad a_k\ge 0, \label{eq:simplex}\\
& |e^{j\phi_i}|=1,\quad i=1,\dots,N . \label{eq:unit}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:qosc} enforces the communication sum-rate floor and
\eqref{eq:qoss} the sensing-rate floor; \eqref{eq:simplex} is the power simplex
and \eqref{eq:unit} the unit-modulus phase constraint. The \emph{sum-rate} form
of~\eqref{eq:qosc} is standard in learning-based RIS/NOMA resource allocation,
where a single cluster-rate floor is enforced as a differentiable penalty during
training rather than a per-user hard constraint~\cite{zou2023ml,wei2026ris,
sun2024noma}; this keeps the learned map on the simplex and the loss smooth. The
formulation extends directly to \emph{individual} per-user floors by replacing
\eqref{eq:qosc} with $R_n\ge R_{\mathrm{th},n}$ and $R_f\ge R_{\mathrm{th},f}$,
which in the meta-training loss~\eqref{eq:loss} corresponds to adding separate
penalty terms $\lambda_n(R_{\mathrm{th},n}-R_n)_+$ and
$\lambda_f(R_{\mathrm{th},f}-R_f)_+$ (indeed our solver already supports these
per-user penalties, with $\lambda_n=\lambda_f=0$ recovering the sum-rate case).
Per-user floors give finer control over the near/far fairness trade-off at the
cost of a smaller feasible region and slightly slower convergence. Problem~(P1)
is non-convex in both the simplex-constrained power split and the unit-modulus
phases, which are moreover coupled through the cascade~\eqref{eq:cascade}.

\section{Proposed MAML-Based Solution}
The central design choice is \emph{what to learn}. The RIS phase vector
$\bm{\phi}\in\mathbb{R}^N$ is high-dimensional and channel-specific, so learning
it directly invites overfitting; the power split $\bm{a}$ is low-dimensional and
admits a stable channel-to-allocation map. We therefore split the variables
across two nested loops and meta-train only the power map.

\subsection{Feature Map and Power-Allocation Network}\label{sec:feat}
From the effective gains we form a compact feature vector
$\bm{z}$ (per-link gains in dB, the QoS margins, and an SNR-budget term), which
a small multilayer perceptron $f_{\bm{\theta}}$ maps to the power split through
a softmax head, $\bm{a}=\mathrm{softmax}(f_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{z}))$, thereby
satisfying the simplex constraint~\eqref{eq:simplex} by construction.

\subsection{MAML-Based Bilevel Optimization}
\subsubsection{Inner Loop -- Per-Channel RIS Phase Refinement}
For each channel realization the phases are refined by $K$ steps of projected
gradient ascent on the objective,
\begin{equation}
\bm{\phi}\leftarrow \bm{\phi}+\eta\,\nabla_{\bm{\phi}}\Rdl(\bm{a},\bm{\phi}),
\quad |e^{j\phi_i}|=1, \label{eq:inner}
\end{equation}
with step size $\eta$. This per-instance refinement is what a one-shot network
cannot reliably reproduce, and it keeps the learned object low-dimensional.

\subsubsection{Outer Loop -- Meta-Training}
The network weights $\bm{\theta}$ are meta-trained across a batch of channels
with the differentiable Lagrangian
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta})=-\,\Eset[\Rdl]
  +\lambda_c\,\Eset[(R_{\mathrm{th},c}-R_n-R_f)_+]
  +\lambda_s\,\Eset[(R_{\mathrm{th},s}-R_s)_+], \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $(\cdot)_+=\max(\cdot,0)$ penalizes QoS violations and
$\lambda_c,\lambda_s$ are penalty factors. Crucially, the meta-gradient is taken
\emph{through} the unrolled inner loop~\eqref{eq:inner} (second-order MAML), so
$\bm{\theta}$ learns power priors that are good \emph{after} phase refinement.

\subsubsection{Training Procedure}
At each iteration a mini-batch of channels is drawn; for each channel the inner
loop~\eqref{eq:inner} refines $\bm{\phi}$ from a random initialization while the
network predicts $\bm{a}$, the loss~\eqref{eq:loss} is accumulated, and
$\bm{\theta}$ is updated by Adam using the second-order meta-gradient. A held-out
validation set selects the best checkpoint. Because the learned object is the
low-dimensional power map, the validation throughput tracks—indeed slightly
exceeds—the training throughput, i.e.\ the model generalizes rather than
memorizes. The overall procedure, which follows the model-agnostic
meta-learning template of~\cite{maml} adapted to the RIS-NOMA-ISAC objective as
in~\cite{zou2023ml}, is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:maml}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\SetAlgoLined
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{channel distribution $\mathcal{D}$; inner steps $K$, step size $\eta$;
meta learning-rate $\alpha$; penalties $\lambda_c,\lambda_s$.}
\KwOut{meta-trained power-map weights $\bm{\theta}^\star$.}
Initialize $\bm{\theta}$\;
\While{not converged}{
  Sample a mini-batch $\{\bG,\bm{r}_n,\bm{r}_f,\bG_T\}_{b=1}^{B}\sim\mathcal{D}$\;
  \ForEach{channel $b$ in the batch}{
    Initialize phases $\bm{\phi}_b\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{Unif}[0,2\pi)^N$\;
    Predict power split $\bm{a}_b=\mathrm{softmax}(f_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{z}_b))$\;
    \For{$k=1$ \KwTo $K$}{
      $\bm{\phi}_b \leftarrow \bm{\phi}_b + \eta\,\nabla_{\bm{\phi}_b}\Rdl(\bm{a}_b,\bm{\phi}_b)$ \tcp*{unrolled inner loop~\eqref{eq:inner}}
    }
    Evaluate rates $R_n,R_f,R_s$ and loss
    $\mathcal{L}_b$ via~\eqref{eq:loss}\;
  }
  $\bm{\theta}\leftarrow\bm{\theta}-\alpha\,\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\tfrac{1}{B}\sum_b\mathcal{L}_b$ \tcp*{2nd-order meta-update through $\bm{\phi}_b$}
}
Select $\bm{\theta}^\star$ by best validation throughput\;
\caption{MAML-based joint power/phase design}
\label{alg:maml}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity}
Training is performed offline; the cost per meta-iteration scales as
$\mathcal{O}(B\,K\,(MN+P_{\bm{\theta}}))$ with batch size $B$, inner steps $K$,
and $P_{\bm{\theta}}$ network parameters. The online stage requires only one
forward pass of $f_{\bm{\theta}}$ plus the $K$ lightweight inner steps, so the
main burden is shifted to offline training—unlike AO, which re-solves the joint
problem for every channel realization.

\section{Numerical Results and Analysis}
Unless otherwise stated, we set $M=4$ antennas and $N=8$ RIS elements, with
weights $\omega_c/\omega_s=0.7/0.3$. Channels follow the Nakagami small-scale
model with distance-dependent path loss; the communication and sensing QoS
floors are set to $R_{\mathrm{th},c}=R_{\mathrm{th},s}=3.1$~bps/Hz, calibrated to
a low-SINR operating regime in which the constraints are active, and the
transmit power is swept over $5$--$25$~dBm. The power-allocation network
$f_{\bm\theta}$ is a three-hidden-layer MLP ($256$ units per layer, LayerNorm and
LeakyReLU, softmax head) fed the compact feature vector $\bm{z}$ of
Sec.~\ref{sec:feat}. It is meta-trained with Adam (learning rate $10^{-4}$, batch
size $64$) for the inner loop of $K{=}5$ projected-gradient steps with step size
$\eta=0.3$; the QoS penalties are $\lambda_c/\lambda_s=2/50$, and results are
reported on a held-out test set of $5{,}000$ channel realizations. We compare the
following schemes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NOMA-RIS (proposed)} and \textbf{NOMA (no-RIS)}: superposition with
SIC, with and without the RIS.
\item \textbf{OMA-RIS} and \textbf{OMA (no-RIS)}: orthogonal time-division
baseline with per-user beamforming.
\item \textbf{Ablation}: \textsf{randPhi\_randAlpha} (no optimization),

Finally, two practical points follow. On \emph{runtime}, the online stage is a
single forward pass of $f_{\bm\theta}$ plus $K{=}5$ light inner steps---orders
of magnitude below per-instance AO---so the design fits the short coherence
times that motivate learning-based ISAC. On \emph{constraint granularity},
replacing the sum-rate floor \eqref{eq:qosc} with per-user penalties
($\lambda_n,\lambda_f{>}0$ in~\eqref{eq:loss}) lets the operator dial the
near/far fairness trade-off, shifting the learned split toward the weaker user
at a modest cost in aggregate rate and with no change to the pipeline.

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
In this letter, a MISO RIS-assisted NOMA-ISAC downlink was investigated. To
maximize a weighted communication-and-sensing throughput under per-link QoS
constraints, we developed a MAML-based DL framework that factorizes the joint
design into a learned low-dimensional power allocation and an unrolled
per-channel RIS phase optimization, coupled by second-order meta-gradients.
Simulation results show that the proposed scheme trains stably and generalizes,
meets the QoS almost surely ($0.1\%$ violation), scales with the RIS aperture,
and consistently outperforms the OMA and no-RIS baselines. Future work includes
multi-cluster NOMA, imperfect SIC/CSI, and a sensing metric beyond the rate
surrogate (e.g.\ the Cram\'er--Rao bound).
